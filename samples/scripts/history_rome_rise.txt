One small city in Italy grew into an empire that ruled the entire Mediterranean.
> map: year=264 BC | view=italy>empire | cities=Rome | label=Rome rises

Then it fought Carthage, in North Africa, for over a century.
> map: year=264 BC | view=west_med | route=Rome > Carthage | label=The Punic Wars

In 218 BC, Hannibal marched elephants over the Alps and nearly broke Rome.
> map: year=218 BC | view=west_med | route=Carthago Nova > Pyrenees > Alps > Po Valley > Cannae | label=Hannibal's march

Rome won anyway. By 133 BC, it held Spain, North Africa, and Greece.
> map: year=133 BC | view=med

Julius Caesar then added Gaul, today's France.
> map: year=44 BC | view=europe | cities=Rome

At its peak in 117 AD, the empire stretched from Britain to Egypt.
> map: year=117 AD | view=wide>empire | label=Greatest extent | note=Shown by modern country. Borders approximate.

Tens of millions of people lived under Roman rule.
> stat: label=People under Roman rule | value=50–70M | sub=Common scholarly estimate at its peak
