Hide {$10,000|ten thousand dollars} in a drawer and it quietly loses almost half its value.
> kinetic: Hide *$10,000* | in a drawer | and it quietly *shrinks*

The thief is inflation. It works like compound interest in reverse.
> stat: label=Illustrative inflation rate | value=3% | sub=of your buying power, gone every year | trend=down

At {3%|three percent} a year, that cash slides to about {$5,500|fifty five hundred dollars} in twenty years.
> line: title=Buying power of $10,000 at 3% inflation | x=Today,5 yrs,10 yrs,15 yrs,20 yrs | y=10000,9709,9426,9151,8885,8626,8375,8131,7894,7664,7441,7224,7014,6810,6611,6419,6232,6050,5874,5703,5537 | prefix=$ | color=bad | delta=-45% | note=Illustrative example. Not financial advice.

So make it earn more than inflation, like a savings account paying {4%|four percent}.
> kinetic: Make cash *earn* | more than | *inflation*

In twenty years, it grows to about {$21,900|twenty one thousand nine hundred dollars}.
> bar: title=Balance after 20 years | x=Drawer,Savings at 4% | y=10000,21900 | prefix=$ | highlight=1 | note=Illustrative example. Not financial advice.

After inflation, it still buys about {$12,100|twelve thousand dollars}. Double the drawer.
> bar: title=Buying power in today's dollars | x=Drawer,Savings at 4% | y=5500,12100 | prefix=$ | bad=0 | highlight=1

Real return is your interest rate minus inflation.
> kinetic: *Real return* | = interest rate | − inflation
