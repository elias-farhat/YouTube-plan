How a Microwave Really Works (It Doesn't Cook From the Inside Out)

DESCRIPTION
A magnetron makes microwaves at 2.45 GHz. The electric field flips about 2.45 billion times a second, and polar water molecules keep trying to turn with it. The twisting and jostling becomes heat. Microwaves only penetrate a few centimeters, so the outer layer heats first and the rest warms by conduction. That is why stirring and resting help, and why microwaves do not cook from the inside out.
