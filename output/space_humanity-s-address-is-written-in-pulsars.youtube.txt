Humanity's Address Is Written in Pulsars

DESCRIPTION
In 1972, NASA bolted a plaque to the Pioneer 10 spacecraft. On it is a map of where we are, made from 14 pulsars: dead stars that spin like lighthouses and keep time almost as well as atomic clocks.

The tick marks beside each line write that pulsar's spin rate in binary. And because pulsars slowly slow down at a known rate, anyone who finds the plaque can work out when it was launched.

The map in this video is a simplified illustration, not the exact plaque.
