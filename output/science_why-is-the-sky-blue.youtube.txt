Why Is the Sky Blue? (And Why Sunsets Are Red)

DESCRIPTION
Sunlight looks white but hides every color. Air molecules scatter short blue wavelengths about 6 times more than long red ones, so blue light reaches your eyes from every direction and the sky looks blue. At sunset the light crosses far more air, most blue is scattered out of the beam, and red and orange survive. (Violet scatters even more, but the Sun emits less of it and our eyes are less sensitive to it.)
