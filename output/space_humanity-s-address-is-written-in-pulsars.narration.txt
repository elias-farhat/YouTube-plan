Humanity's Address Is Written in Pulsars

Humanity's address is written in pulsars.

A pulsar is a dead star spinning so fast that its beam sweeps past like a lighthouse.

Some spin hundreds of times a second, and keep time almost as well as an atomic clock.

In 1972, NASA bolted a plaque to the Pioneer 10 spacecraft.

On it, fourteen lines point from the Sun to fourteen pulsars, and tick marks write each spin rate in binary.

But pulsars slow down at a known rate. So whoever finds the plaque can work out when it was launched.

No language needed. The stars give our address, and the date.
