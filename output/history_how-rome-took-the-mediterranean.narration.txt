How Rome Took The Mediterranean

One city took the whole Mediterranean.

In 264 BC, Rome ruled only Italy.

Then it fought Carthage, a rival power across the sea, for over a century.

In 218 BC, Hannibal marched elephants over the Alps and nearly broke Rome.

Rome won anyway. By 133 BC, it held Spain, North Africa, and Greece.

Julius Caesar then added Gaul, today's France.

At its peak in 117 AD, the empire stretched from Britain to Egypt.

Tens of millions of people lived under Roman rule.
